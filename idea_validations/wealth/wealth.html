01 — The before

The advice you can afford doesn’t know how you get paid.

Priced wrong

Advice is either generic and cheap, or personal and priced for the already-wealthy. If your net worth is mostly unvested equity, you are in the wide middle: too complex for a basic robo-advisor, and too small or too non-standard for private wealth. A dashboard of tickers does not make that gap smaller.

Wrong defaults

Products assume steady W-2 income, one employer, and simple taxes. Your year is vests, windows, ESPP purchases, maybe a tender or a job change. The tools stay fragmented across the broker, the tax software, and the benefits portal. The mistake happens in the gap, usually on a deadline you did not set.

Quiet leakage

Fees and conflicts stay opaque. AUM pricing punishes people for saving more, and it still will not sit with you through concentration. Behavior and life admin — taxes, equity comp, irregular cash around a vest, a household that never sees the same calendar — cause more wealth leakage than fund selection. The expensive error is the one you cannot undo after the window closes.

Why the last fix didn’t hold

Past solutions failed because they were built for a paycheck and a fund menu. Generic advice ignores how tech and public-company pay actually works. An “equity” tab is not a compensation system. A private-bank pitch is not a plan if the price of entry is already being wealthy. Lead forms that say “talk to an advisor” do not publish a price, a specialty, or a boundary. You did not fail at finance. The product assumed a life you do not have.

02 — The after

Imagine the vest is a plan, not a surprise.

Relief

You can see the shape before you click

A tax-estimate scenario is on the calendar before the window, in language you can take to a CPA. Nobody texts you a hot tip. Nobody promises a best sale price. The relief is knowing what the click interacts with — withholding, estimated taxes, the rest of the year — while you can still wait.

Agency

Concentration is a rule you wrote

Employer stock has a cap you set, not a vibe you revisit after a rally. Drift shows up as a policy miss, not as a surprise in a brokerage pie chart. Diversification is something you accept or reject. The account does not move itself.

Steadiness

The life event has a checklist

IPO, tender, layoff, or a new offer no longer means four logins and a thread with your partner at midnight. The household can see what to revisit. The plan updates when pay structure changes, which is when static advice goes stale.

The better way is not a smarter fund menu. It is depth in one compensation system: the calendar and the tax interaction, priced as a subscription rather than a percent of assets, sitting on accounts you already have. Software prepares the map, the scenario, and the checklist. A human — you, your CPA, or an advisor you choose — approves anything irreversible. That is the opposite of set-and-forget.

03 — The product

Equity Comp Navigator

A specialist system for RSUs, options, ESPPs, trading windows, and single-stock concentration. Built so fewer tax and concentration mistakes happen in the dark — by making them visible early, not by picking a sale for you.

  1. 1

    Put the grants on one calendar

    Vests, windows, blackout periods, IPO or tender dates, and the job-change cliff. Every default is for equity pay, not a W-2 app with a side mode. You stay at your broker. This is not a new bank and not custody.

  2. 2

    Read the scenario, then set the policy

    Tax-estimate ranges and a written diversification rule: an employer-stock cap, a cash minimum, what you refuse to decide inside a window. You accept or reject. Nothing trades in the background. There is an audit trail of what you were shown.

  3. 3

    Run the checklist, and stop for a human

    A planning intensive only when a vest or liquidity event is actually on the calendar. The first version is education, calendars, and scenario templates with clear advice boundaries. Where a decision needs a license, the product stops and points you to a person who has one.

I kept meeting people who were wealthy on a vesting schedule and lost in the admin. The robo wanted a paycheck. Private wealth wanted a bigger account. Neither would open a trading window, show the tax interaction, and then stop before giving advice they were not licensed to give.

Equity Comp Navigator is the narrow version on purpose. One compensation system. A policy you can read. A human stop. It will not sell for you, and it will not name a best price. It exists so the irreversible mistake is harder to make by accident.

— Founder, Equity Comp Navigator
