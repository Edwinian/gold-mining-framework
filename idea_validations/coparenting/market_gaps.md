The useful opportunity is not “a better coparenting app.” The thread’s unmet need is narrower: stay logistical, pass safety-critical facts, and do not require friendship. Most existing tools fail that brief because they assume two willing partners and a shared ideal of matching homes.

Treat everything below as hypothesis generation from one thread, not market proof. Frequency in that discussion is not prevalence. A product cannot make an unwilling or abusive parent cooperate, and it becomes actively harmful if it is designed as if both adults will log in good faith.

Design constraints that should filter every concept:
- Default to parallel parenting, not cooperative coparenting. Week 3 is not year 2.
- Value must exist if only one parent participates.
- Separate a safety-fact channel (medication, school, medical) from any relationship channel.
- Kids are not users of the conflict layer.
- No covert monitoring of the other household. The job is a record of what was sent, received, and withheld — not surveillance.
- Do not sell “less abuse.” Sell a narrower record and a lower-conflict operating mode, and route real safety issues to solicitors, mediators, and child-protection professionals.

## Executive Summary

The market gap is a mismatch between the story people are sold (two homes, one team) and the operating reality (raw, needs-only, often one-sided). Conflict is produced by forcing alignment too early, by conflating coparenting with parallel parenting, and by tools that go dark when the other adult will not show up.

Three solution themes follow from that:

1. **Mode, not morale.** A staged system that starts in parallel / needs-only and only later offers a cooperative mode.
2. **Asymmetric utility.** The parent who is actually doing the work gets a complete record and a calmer workflow even if the other parent never logs in, never pays, and never agrees.
3. **A thin safety pipe.** Medication, medical, and school facts move on a separate, exportable channel that does not pretend to be co-parenting.

The strongest near-term business is a parallel-first operating system sold through solicitors and mediators, priced so one parent can carry it. The highest-intensity pain — information blackout, including medication, inside a high-conflict dynamic — is a real wedge only as a narrow, one-sided, court-exportable ledger. It is a bad wedge if packaged as a relationship product.

## 1. Market Segmentation Framework

### Concept: The First Ninety Days

A short-horizon operating kit for parents in the first weeks after separation, when divorce paperwork may not even be accepted and the only workable talk is needs-only.

It does not ask “how should we coparent?” It gives a temporary protocol: what must be shared (pickup, medication, school), what is out of scope (tone, routines, the relationship), and a dated reminder to revisit in 90 days rather than renegotiate every handoff. Communication is templated and limited. There is no shared vision board and no expectation of matching bedtimes.

**Key features:** Needs-only message templates; a 90-day freeze on “how it should be” talks; a visible mode label (“parallel for now”); a revisit date both can ignore without penalty.

**Value proposition:** Stability without a premature peace treaty.

**Business model:** Low one-time fee or a 90-day pass, often bought by one parent or bundled by a solicitor at the start of proceedings. Upsell into a longer parallel-parenting plan only if they are still in it at day 90.

**Pain points addressed:** The week-3 mess — transactional, raw, divorce not accepted, unable to open the big conversation. It treats “come back in a year” as a product state, not a brush-off.

**Target audience:** Newly separated parents, especially the one trying not to make a raw split worse. UK separation is a plausible first slice because the thread’s logistics (divorce application not yet accepted) are jurisdiction-specific, but that is a hypothesis, not a finding.

**Challenges:** People in week 3 are poor customers; they are exhausted and not shopping for software. Acquisition has to sit where they already are — the solicitor, the mediator, the court form — not in an app store. A 90-day product also churns by design, so the business only works if the handoff to a longer plan is clean.

**Best-in-category potential:** Moderate. It can be the clearest “do not coparent yet” product on the market. It will not be a large standalone company. Its power is as the front door to a longer system, and as the thing incumbents are culturally unable to say because their brand is cooperation.

### Concept: The Primary Desk

A workspace for the parent who still does school, medical, and daily care after the other adult has moved far away or effectively exited, while visitation is rare.

The product assumes a 90/10 labor split, not a 50/50 partnership. The nearby parent keeps the system of record. The distant parent gets a narrow window: upcoming visits, medical and school facts, and a way to acknowledge receipt. There is no pretense of joint routines.

**Key features:** One-owner record; visit planning that does not require weekly coordination; a one-way update stream for school and medical; exportable history of what was sent.

**Value proposition:** Stop calling solo parenting “coparenting,” and stop losing the paper trail while you do it.

**Business model:** Subscription paid by the primary parent. The distant parent is free, so non-payment cannot shut the record down.

**Pain points addressed:** The parent eight hours away who still has to inform on medical and school while the other shows up once or twice a year. It does not fix the labor imbalance. It makes the imbalance legible and the updates survivable.

**Target audience:** The on-the-ground parent in long-distance or disengaged arrangements. Secondary user is a low-engagement parent who will not adopt a “team” app but might open a link.

**Challenges:** The real problem is distance and withdrawal, which software cannot move. Willingness to pay is uncertain: this parent is already carrying the cost of everything else. If the distant parent feels surveilled, they will reject it — so the product must show only what the primary parent chose to send, not a feed of the child’s life.

**Best-in-category potential:** Narrow but real. No major tool is honest that some “coparenting” is solo parenting with a notification list. Category dominance only inside that sub-niche, not in coparenting generally.

### Concept: Unwilling-Partner Record

A one-sided safety and legal record for a parent who shares legal custody with someone who withholds what happened on “their” time, including medication, and who will not use a shared app in good faith.

The willing parent logs what they communicated, when, and whether there was any acknowledgment. They can attach what they were told by the child or by a school or clinic, clearly marked as secondhand. The other parent may be invited; their silence is itself a timestamped fact. The product does not ask them to confess, co-author a story, or be observed inside their home.

**Key features:** Outbound message log with delivery and read status; a medication-communication checklist (“dose change sent,” “no acknowledgment”); export formatted for a solicitor; explicit labels for firsthand vs reported information; no hidden location, audio, or in-home monitoring.

**Value proposition:** A calm, court-usable record of attempts to share safety-critical facts — without requiring a partner.

**Business model:** Paid by the participating parent, or by a solicitor as a matter-management add-on. Free invite for the other party so refusal to pay is not a veto.

**Pain points addressed:** The highest-intensity case in the thread: shared legal custody, information blackout, medication handled on the other parent’s time, and a goal of reducing harm rather than building a friendship. It does not lessen abuse. It preserves a record and lowers the chance that safety facts live only in angry texts.

**Target audience:** The protective parent in a high-conflict or parallel arrangement where the other adult rejects transparency. Not for couples who simply disagree about screen time.

**Challenges:** Liability and trust. A log of secondhand reports can be misused as ammunition and can pull the child into adult conflict if the parent treats the child as the sensor. Design has to discourage “what did Daddy do?” harvesting. Courts and solicitors must actually accept the export, or the product is a diary with a price tag. An unwilling parent can also flood it with noise. This is the concept most likely to attract buyers and the concept most likely to cause harm if scoped loosely.

**Best-in-category potential:** High inside a grim niche — “the record that still works when they will not cooperate” — and low as a consumer brand. Incumbents such as court-ordered messaging tools already own “unalterable record” when a judge orders both sides on. The open lane is the case where no order exists yet and only one side will participate. Dominance depends on solicitor acceptance, not on features.

## 2. Product Differentiation Framework

### Concept: Parallel OS

A full operating system whose default mode is parallel parenting: strict plan, minimal communication, each house runs itself, joint decisions only for medical, education, and agreed activities.

Coparenting mode exists, but it is opt-in later, not the homepage. The product teaches the distinction on day one and refuses to call inconsistent houses a failure.

**Key features:** Mode switch (parallel now / cooperative later); a short “joint decisions only” list; house-level routines that are private, not synced; a conflict brake that limits threads to logistics unless both sides open a decision item.

**Value proposition:** The first system that treats parallel parenting as a legitimate product, not a downgrade.

**Business model:** Mid-priced annual subscription. One payer unlocks both sides.

**Pain points addressed:** Definition mismatch that turns into arguments; parents who want cooperation later but are correctly in parallel now; rules that blur across houses, by making “same rules” optional rather than the measure of success.

**Target audience:** Separated parents who are not in crisis but are tired of tools and relatives that shame them for not being a team. Includes the “cordial, not friends” families who already have a working setup and want it formalized.

**Challenges:** “Parallel” is a worse marketing word than “coparent.” Category creation means educating buyers who searched for the wrong term. Feature creep back toward a social coparenting app will destroy the point.

**Best-in-category potential:** The clearest shot at a new category name. Not “best coparenting app.” Best parallel-parenting system. Sustainable only if the mode model is structural — permissions, templates, and onboarding — rather than a label on a normal shared calendar.

### Concept: Safety Pipe

A deliberately small product: medication, allergies, clinicians, school contacts, and urgent logistics. Nothing else. No chat about feelings, no photo feed, no shared holidays.

Messages are short, timestamped, and exportable. A change in medication is a structured event, not a paragraph in a thread that also contains resentment.

**Key features:** Structured medication and clinician cards; change log; acknowledgment request without a reply thread; emergency-only escalation template that points to real-world services rather than in-app argument.

**Value proposition:** The facts that can hurt a child move even when the adults cannot talk.

**Business model:** Low subscription or a per-matter fee. Could be free to the second parent and paid by clinics or schools as a referral tool, though that channel is slow.

**Pain points addressed:** Medication withholding and one-way medical/school informing. It does not detect what happened in the other house. It makes non-response visible and keeps the safety facts out of the relationship argument.

**Target audience:** Any split where medical or school information is the actual stake — high-conflict parents, long-distance primaries, and early-separation parents who can agree on nothing else.

**Challenges:** Too narrow to feel like a company, unless it is a module inside Parallel OS. Clinicians will not adopt another portal quickly. Parents may still hide information; a pipe does not create honesty. Over-collecting health data creates privacy and child-safeguarding obligations that a small team can mishandle.

**Best-in-category potential:** High as a feature, moderate as a standalone. “Best medication handoff for split homes” is a winnable phrase. It becomes category-defining only if solicitors and pediatric settings recognize the export. It should not claim to make dosing safe.

### Concept: The Minimum Agreement

A facilitated, very short decision tool that forces an honest classification: either you agree on a handful of basics (bedtime, screens, medication, pickup) or you formally select parallel parenting and stop litigating the rest.

Output is a one-page plan, not a parenting philosophy. Disagreement is a valid result. The artifact says which items are joint, which are house-rules, and which are “do not discuss.”

**Key features:** Ten-item max; explicit “we do not agree, so this is house-level” outcome; plain-language definitions of coparenting vs parallel parenting; a PDF a mediator can put in the file.

**Value proposition:** End the fight about what coparenting is supposed to mean by writing down the actual mode.

**Business model:** One-time purchase, or included in a mediator’s session fee. White-label for mediation practices.

**Pain points addressed:** Skewed expectations; the category error of calling disagreement on basics “failed coparenting”; early blurring of rules, by naming the blur instead of pretending it is temporary rudeness.

**Target audience:** Recently separated parents and the mediators or solicitors sitting between them. Also useful for the parent who cannot articulate the situation in one post and needs a structure.

**Challenges:** Easy to copy. A PDF is not a moat. It only becomes a business if distribution owns the moment of plan-writing. Some high-conflict pairs will turn the ten items into ten new fights; the tool needs a “stop, this is parallel” exit ramp.

**Best-in-category potential:** Low as software, high as the onboarding standard others lack. Best used as the front end of Parallel OS, not as a company.

## 3. Business Model Innovation Framework

### Concept: One Payer, Both Doors

Not a new feature set. A commercial rule: one parent can buy the system; the other parent never has to pay, never has to “join a family plan,” and cannot cancel it.

Refusal to subscribe is the classic way a hostile or disengaged coparent kills a tool. Removing that veto is the product.

**Key features:** Single-payer license; permanent free seat for the other adult; records remain exportable if they never appear; no “upgrade to unlock the other parent.”

**Value proposition:** The parent who wants a paper trail is not held hostage by the parent who does not.

**Business model:** Higher annual price to the willing parent, offset by not needing two subscriptions to function. Solicitor bulk licenses. Possible employer/EAP benefit for the employee who is the participating parent.

**Pain points addressed:** Unwilling partner, disengaged distant parent, and early-separation cases where only one person is ready to systematize. Directly answers the warning that a product fails if it assumes both parties will participate.

**Target audience:** The more organized or more frightened parent. Buyers may also be solicitors who are tired of clients whose apps died because the ex would not pay.

**Challenges:** Unit economics. Coparenting subscriptions are already a hard sell; charging one person more can feel unfair. The free seat can be abused to harass. Abuse prevention has to be in the product (rate limits, logistics-only templates, blocking that still preserves the record) without becoming a surveillance suite.

**Best-in-category potential:** High as a business-model distinction. Several court tools are effectively two-party. “Works when they won’t pay and won’t engage” is a durable line if the record quality holds up. Easy to copy once proven, so the moat is channel relationships, not the pricing page.

### Concept: Matter File, Not an App

Sell to the professional, not the parent. A solicitor or mediator runs a matter workspace: mode selection, the one-page agreement, the safety pipe, and an export that drops into the case file. Parents get a simple client view.

**Key features:** Professional console; client seats that do not require the parents to “become a team”; chronology export; templates aligned to parenting-plan language; audit trail of what the professional sent the clients to do.

**Value proposition:** Fewer chaotic email forwards, and a plan that matches the actual conflict level.

**Business model:** Per-matter fee to the practice, passed through to the client as a disbursement. This matches how people already pay for separation help, and it reaches them when the pain is acute.

**Pain points addressed:** Early-separation logistics that parents cannot discuss cleanly; definition mismatch, because the professional sets the mode; high-conflict documentation, because the file is built for someone who will actually read an export.

**Target audience:** Family solicitors and mediators first. Parents are users, not the economic buyer. Geography should follow where you can sit in a real professional workflow; do not assume a US court-app playbook transfers.

**Challenges:** Long sales cycles, professional indemnity concerns, and implementation drag. Practices are conservative. If the tool looks like it practices law, it will be rejected. Integration with whatever the firm already uses matters more than consumer polish.

**Best-in-category potential:** Strong. Consumer coparenting apps are crowded and assume cooperation. A professional matter file that defaults to parallel parenting is a less crowded shelf. Category dominance is local and relational — a default in a region’s family-law practices — not global app-store rank.

### Concept: The Revisit

A scheduled, optional service: every six or twelve months, a structured check on whether parallel mode is still right, or whether a few items can become joint. No one is graded. Skipping the revisit is a valid outcome.

**Key features:** Annual session, human or tightly scripted; comparison against the original one-page plan; a written “stay parallel” or “add these three joint items” result; no social feed in between.

**Value proposition:** Time, which the thread says is required, becomes a calendar event instead of a vague hope.

**Business model:** Low retainer or a yearly session fee. High margin, low frequency. Works as an attachment to Parallel OS, not alone.

**Pain points addressed:** “Come back in a year.” Parents who want coparenting eventually but know forcing it now creates conflict. Also the fatigue of not knowing whether the current mess is the permanent mode.

**Target audience:** Parents past the raw phase who are stable enough to look up. Not week-3 buyers.

**Challenges:** Easy to skip, so retention is soft. A human facilitator is better and more expensive. Done badly, the revisit reopens wounds the parallel plan had closed. Must be allowed to end in “no change.”

**Best-in-category potential:** Modest. It is a retention and outcomes layer, not a category. Best in the world only as the review ritual attached to a mode-based system.

## 4. Distribution and Marketing Framework

### Concept: Solicitor Counter Card

Distribution is the product. A one-page leave-behind and a client onboarding link that a family practice hands out at the first separation meeting: definitions, the 90-day protocol, and the one-payer record.

Marketing claim is deliberately unromantic: you do not have to like your business partner; you do have to move medication and pickup times without a fight.

**Key features:** Practice-branded handout; QR to a client workspace; short script the solicitor can say without adopting the client’s conflict; no consumer social campaign required.

**Value proposition:** Reach people in week 3, when they will not search for an app and should not be sold friendship.

**Business model:** Free to the parent via the practice; the practice pays per matter or gets it inside the professional console. Content is the acquisition channel, not a media business.

**Pain points addressed:** Early raw communication, and the idea-versus-reality gap, at the moment those pains are being created.

**Target audience:** Family solicitors and their newly separated clients. Secondary: mediators.

**Challenges:** Channel concentration. A few practices can sink the pipeline. Compliance with how legal services may be marketed. The handout will be ignored if it looks like an app ad rather than a way to reduce the solicitor’s inbox.

**Best-in-category potential:** High leverage, low glamour. The winning coparenting-adjacent product in this niche may never win an app-store category. It wins by being the thing the practice already hands out.

### Concept: School and Clinic Handoff

A partnership, not an ad channel. Schools and pediatric clinics are where one-way informing already happens — the exact labor the distant or excluded parent still triggers. Offer them a single “separated homes” send path so staff are not choosing which parent to email, and parents are not using the school as the messenger for adult conflict.

**Key features:** Staff sends a factual notice once; both parents receive it, or the participating parent receives it and the send to the other is logged; no parent-to-parent argument thread inside the school’s tool; medication and attendance stay structured.

**Value proposition:** Institutions stop being the unwilling coparenting platform.

**Business model:** Institution pays a small annual fee, or a pilot is free and the parent product converts the family. Sales cycle is long. This is a year-two channel, not a launch channel.

**Pain points addressed:** One-way medical and school updates; information blackout, by creating a third-party factual source that does not depend on the ex’s mood.

**Target audience:** School administrators and clinic managers first; the primary parent is the beneficiary.

**Challenges:** Safeguarding, consent, and data protection. Schools will not want a vendor that escalates parental conflict. Integration with existing student systems is the real work. A startup that leads with this channel will starve.

**Best-in-category potential:** High if achieved, unlikely as a first move. Whoever becomes the boring default for “notify both homes” has a durable position. That is a distribution moat, not a feature moat.

### Concept: Parallel Is Not Failure

A content and community front end that teaches the distinction the thread’s commenters keep having to explain: coparenting versus parallel parenting, cordial not friends, joint decisions only where they matter, inconsistency as the price of less conflict.

The content does not coach people on how to win against an ex. It coaches them on which operating mode they are actually in, and it routes high-conflict safety issues to professional help rather than to comment-section tactics.

**Key features:** Short diagnostic; plain-language guides; templates; a moderated space with strict logistics-only rules; a path into the paid mode system. No war stories as a growth engine.

**Value proposition:** The search query people actually have — “are we failing at coparenting?” — answered without selling them a fantasy.

**Business model:** Content acquires; the OS or the one-page agreement converts. Community is a cost center with a hard moderation budget, not a viral bet.

**Pain points addressed:** Definition gap and the shame of not matching homes. Indirectly, early-separation parents who need permission to stay transactional.

**Target audience:** The parent who is still figuring it out and is about to pick the wrong tool because the right word was “parallel.”

**Challenges:** This audience is hurt and angry. A community will rot into ex-bashing unless moderation is expensive and ruthless. Content alone is not a moat; incumbents can publish the same definitions. Do not build this as a media company.

**Best-in-category potential:** Good acquisition wedge, poor standalone business. Best used to own the language (“parallel first”) that the product then enforces.

## 5. New Paradigm Framework

### Concept: Asymmetric System of Record

The category shift: coparenting software has been a two-player game. This is a one-player system that can become two-player, never the reverse.

The participating parent’s outbound facts, decisions, and requests are the record. The other adult’s participation is optional enrichment, not a dependency. Silence, refusal, and non-acknowledgment are data. Invention is not: the system must not infer what happened in the other home.

**Key features:** Single-party completeness; invitation without dependency; distinction between observed, sent, and reported; export that a professional can read; no covert collection.

**Value proposition:** A record that does not collapse when the partnership is fictional.

**Business model:** One-payer subscription, distributed through practices. The paradigm is the lock-in: once a year of chronology sits there, switching costs are real.

**Pain points addressed:** All three top pains — unwilling partner, early one-sided logistics, and distant disengaged parent — share one structure: bilateral tools assume a bilateral reality.

**Target audience:** The parent who already knows the other adult will not meet them halfway. Professionals who need a chronology before any court order forces both parties onto an incumbent platform.

**Challenges:** “Asymmetric” can slide into a case-building weapon or into surveillance. The line has to be productized: you may record what you sent and what you were officially told; you may not be sold tools to monitor the other home, the other adult’s devices, or the child as an informant. Legal admissibility varies by jurisdiction and cannot be promised from a feature list. Incumbents can add a one-sided mode if the idea works.

**Best-in-category potential:** This is the actual category opportunity. Not a better shared calendar. The system of record for splits that are not partnerships. Defensible only with professional acceptance and a hard anti-surveillance scope. If those slip, it is neither best nor safe.

### Concept: Mode as the Product

Stop selling features. Sell a state machine: Raw (days to months) → Parallel (default, may be permanent) → Selective joint decisions → Cooperative, only if both opt in.

Each state has different permissions. Raw state cannot open a “parenting philosophy” thread. Parallel state hides house routines from the other home. Cooperative state is a promotion you both have to accept, not a setting one person turns on.

**Key features:** Enforced state permissions; a revisit gate between states; language that never calls parallel a failure; house privacy by default.

**Value proposition:** The product embodies the timeline people are already living, instead of arguing with it.

**Business model:** Subscription that is allowed to stay in parallel forever. Revenue does not depend on “upgrading” couples into friendship. That alignment matters: a business that only profits when parents become cooperative will push them there too early.

**Pain points addressed:** Raw early separation; the false choice between coparenting and chaos; conflict created by skewed expectations.

**Target audience:** Anyone entering a separation who might otherwise buy a cooperative app and abandon it in week 3. Especially parents who already know they want parallel now and maybe something warmer later.

**Challenges:** State machines feel abstract in a sales conversation. The value has to show up as “this app will not make you have that conversation yet.” Competitors can copy labels; they will struggle to copy it if their brand, templates, and pricing all assume cooperation.

**Best-in-category potential:** Highest “best in its category” score, because the category is currently mis-specified. The winner is the product that makes parallel the default without apology. That is a positioning and architecture win, not a technology win.

### Concept: Third-Party Fact Layer

A longer-term play: safety-critical facts enter from institutions (school, clinic, activity) rather than from the ex. Parents subscribe to facts instead of trusting each other to report them.

This is not a parent-tracking network. It is opt-in, institutional, and limited to categories both the institution and the legal framework already treat as shareable with people who hold parental responsibility.

**Key features:** Institution-originated events; parent acknowledgment separate from parent-authored narrative; clear consent and safeguarding rules; no location trailing, no in-home data, no child social feed.

**Value proposition:** The fact does not depend on an unwilling adult’s honesty.

**Business model:** Institution contracts plus a parent account. Slow, regulated, and only viable after a parent-side product already has distribution.

**Pain points addressed:** Medication and school blackout; one-way informing when a parent has moved away. It still cannot reveal what a parent chooses to do at home and does not tell a clinic.

**Target audience:** Schools, clinics, and the parent shut out of information. Not a consumer launch audience.

**Challenges:** Privacy law, safeguarding, and the risk of building a child-data business that outruns its purpose. Institutional sales are a different company from a parent app. If consent is murky, do not build it.

**Best-in-category potential:** High ceiling, low near-term feasibility. This is the five-year version of Safety Pipe. It is the only concept that reduces reliance on the unwilling parent’s self-report without crossing into covert surveillance. It is also the easiest concept to build irresponsibly.

## Opportunity Assessment

Ranked on market size and growth, sustainability of advantage, feasibility, and the chance of being best in a real category. Intensity of pain is not the same as quality of opportunity. The medication/abuse case is the hottest pain and a dangerous thing to build around unless the scope stays narrow.

### 1. Mode as the Product, packaged as Parallel OS, sold One Payer through solicitors

**Why first.** It matches the most repeated pain (raw, needs-only, not ready) and the highest-leverage educational pain (coparenting versus parallel) without pretending to fix an unwilling or abusive adult. Market size is the broad separated-parent population, not a tiny crisis niche, and the buyer can be the practice rather than the exhausted parent. Advantage is sustainable only as a bundle: default-parallel architecture, one-payer economics, and a professional channel. Any one of those alone will be copied. Feasibility is high if version one is templates, a one-page plan, a logistics log, and a mode gate — not AI, not school integrations, not a community. Category dominance is plausible in a category you name (“parallel-first separation system”), not in “coparenting apps,” which is crowded and pointed at the wrong job.

**Do not.** Brand it as coparenting. Gate revenue on couples becoming cooperative. Launch consumer-first and hope solicitors arrive.

### 2. Asymmetric System of Record, with Safety Pipe as the first module

**Why second.** It is the only concept that takes the highest-intensity pain seriously without requiring the other adult’s good faith. Competitive advantage is real if solicitors accept the export and if the product is visibly not a spyware tool; incumbents own the court-ordered two-party lane, not the pre-order one-party lane. Feasibility is medium: the software is simple, the trust and liability design is not. Market size is smaller than all separated parents and must not be inflated from one comment. “Best in the world” is available only as “the record that still works when they will not participate,” and only if secondhand child reports are tightly constrained so the child does not become the data source.

**Do not.** Promise safety, promise admissibility, infer what happened on the other parent’s time, or add location, audio, or device monitoring. If the buyer wants those, this is the wrong company.

### 3. The Minimum Agreement plus The First Ninety Days, as the professional leave-behind

**Why third.** Fastest to implement, directly attacks the definition gap and the week-3 timing error, and is the cheapest test of whether solicitors will distribute anything. Market size is decent because it attaches to separation itself, which is not a fad. Advantage is weak alone — a PDF is copyable — so this ranks third as a business and first as a validation instrument. If practices will not hand it out, the larger OS should not be built. Category dominance is unlikely; it is the wedge, not the company.

**Do not.** Build the school/clinic fact layer, the community, or the annual human revisit until this leave-behind is in use. Those are real options later. They are not the opportunity hiding in this evidence.

---

**What this evidence does not support building yet:** a social coparenting network, a feature-complete calendar competitor, anything whose success requires both parents to log honestly, and any tool aimed at catching an ex inside their own home. The labor imbalance of a parent eight hours away is mostly not a software problem; serve it only as a one-sided update desk inside the same system.

**Validation before build,** still inside the brief you already set: talk to family solicitors and mediators about whether they would hand out a parallel-first protocol, and find threads or interviews about failed apps, exchanges, money, and medical handoffs. Until those exist, the ranked bet is a narrow professional pilot — 90-day parallel protocol, one-page mode agreement, one-payer logistics log — not a platform.
