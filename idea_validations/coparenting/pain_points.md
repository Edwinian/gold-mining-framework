Pain Point Analysis Summary

This is one r/coparenting thread, not a market sample. Most comments define coparenting or describe a working setup. The actual pain is concentrated in a few voices: a parent three weeks into separation who cannot get past needs-only logistics, a parent carrying almost all the work because the other parent moved away, and a parent trying to limit harm from an unwilling coparent who withholds information, including medication. A smaller theme is that “coparenting” and “parallel parenting” get conflated, and that mismatch itself creates conflict.

Do not treat frequency notes below as market prevalence. They describe this thread only.

---

## Category: Early separation — raw, transactional, not ready to “coparent”

### The split is too new for real coparenting conversations
Newly separated parents are stuck in needs-only communication while divorce logistics are unfinished. They may want a cooperative model later, but forcing that conversation now feels impossible.

- “It's all very transactional and raw right now. My STBEX is generally operating on needs only coms.”
- “She hasn't even accepted the Divorce application (UK) yet.”
- “Initially she talked about being awesome co-parents... I just don't think we are in that place yet. Just figuring out our own routines.”
- “So it's messy right now. iMO. And hard to open up conversations about how it should be. I think time is needed without conflict which is all we are doing right now”
- “That's because right now on week 3 we really haven't figured all this out it's very new and raw to both of us so it's about having space keeping kids loved and cared for.”

Frequency/Intensity: High within this thread (the original poster returns to it repeatedly). Language is “transactional,” “raw,” “messy,” not explosive. Urgency is about not making it worse, not about a tool failing.

### People default to parallel parenting even when they want coparenting
The desired end state and the current operating mode do not match. Parents protect the kids’ stability by not pushing alignment.

- “I know the difference between the two and we are more like parraell parenting mode without any conflict. I think we both would like to co-parent but I think the split is still raw so rather than forcing anything we are focusing on kids stability and comfort with the 50/50 share of care”
- “Very hard to articulate everything on a single post”
- “Come back in a year. Bring the other co-parent.”

Frequency/Intensity: Moderate. One parent states it directly; another commenter treats “come back in a year” as the realistic timeline. Intensity is fatigue and uncertainty, not rage.

## Category: Definition gap — idea vs. reality

### Skewed expectations about what coparenting is create conflict
People enter separation with a shared-parenting ideal that does not match how the other parent will actually behave. That gap is described as a cause of arguments, not just confusion.

- “I think the idea and the reality of co-parenting is massively skewed in many minds. This creates conflict in people and ultimately leads to arguments.”
- “There is a reason you couldn't stay together parenting approaches and styles may have played a big part.”
- “Recently joined the club and still figuring things out.”
- “There's coparenting and then there's parallel parenting. Coparenting means working together and trying to maintain general consistency between homes. ... Parallel parenting means you do your thing at your house, and ex does their thing at their house. Minimal communication, reserved for the necessities. Adhering to the parenting plan strictly. Usually needed in high conflict situations or situations where one parent is significantly crappier at parenting but not to the point it's illegal.”

Frequency/Intensity: The original post is built on this. Other commenters answer by defining terms rather than describing their own failure, so the pain is specific but not widely echoed in first person. Solvable in part by clearer models, not by assuming everyone can reach the cooperative version.

### Parents who cannot agree on basics are not actually coparenting
Disagreement on routines is framed as a category error: if you cannot align on bedtime and screen time, you are parallel parenting, and different houses will feel inconsistent to the child.

- “her and her ex do not agree on most things around parenting and so they parallel parent which looks very different than coparenting. In a case where you are parallel parenting that really can mean different routines at each house which is challenging but can lead to less conflict.”
- “I don’t think any coparents parent EXACTLY the same way but there are certain routines like bedtimes, screentime, etc that they probably agree on. If they don’t agree on the basics, then they are parallel parenting not coparenting.”
- “Generally following the same rules we had when living together but I know on both sides those lines have blurred.”

Frequency/Intensity: Moderate. “Challenging” is the emotional word; commenters also say less conflict can be worth the inconsistency. This is a tradeoff pain, not a crisis.

## Category: The other parent will not show up as a partner

### Negative feelings toward the ex override the kids
The stated failure mode of coparenting is not logistics. It is one or both adults refusing to put the child above resentment.

- “I agree that co-parenting means the kids are happy in both homes, and that both parents communicate and work together to do what is best for the kids. Unfortunately a lot of people are unable to put the kids above their negative feelings towards the other parent, and that causes so many problems for the kids.”

Frequency/Intensity: One comment, but it is the clearest general diagnosis in the thread. High implied intensity (“so many problems for the kids”), low specificity — no incident, age, or behavior named.

### Distance turns “coparenting” into solo parenting with occasional visits
When one parent relocates, the label stops matching the labor. The nearby parent still does the informing; the far parent gets rare visitation.

- “My ex moved 8 hrs away from his kids so the thought of coparenting doesn’t make sense to me. I inform him of any medical and school related issues but I’m basically doing all the parenting while he gets his visitation 1-2x a year.”
- “IMO coparenting is basically a 50:50 partnership with both parents to take care, participate in activities and the well being of a child.”

Frequency/Intensity: One detailed firsthand case. High specificity (8 hours, 1–2 visits a year, medical and school updates still flowing one way). Emotional tone is resigned more than desperate.

### Unwilling partner, information blackout, and unsafe medication handling
The sharpest pain in the thread: shared legal custody without shared information, including medication given on the other parent’s time, plus an explicit goal of reducing emotional abuse from the other home.

- “My version of coparenting is trying to lessen the extent of emotional abuse and impact from the other home, and helping my child reach adulthood safely (and hopefully well-adjusted.)”
- “It's really parallel parenting, but we share legal custody so I'm trying my hardest with an unwilling partner who believes anything that occurs on "his" time means I don't need to know about it (including him dosing medication, which is infuriating and dangerous.)”

Frequency/Intensity: One commenter, highest intensity in the data (“infuriating and dangerous,” “emotional abuse,” “safely”). This is a safety and control problem, not a scheduling annoyance. A product cannot fix an unwilling or abusive coparent; it can only fail if it assumes both parties will log honestly.

## What people describe wanting (contrast, not success stories)

These are not pain points. They show the bar some families say they have cleared, which clarifies the gap for everyone else:

- Joint decisions only on medical, education, and extracurriculars; each home runs itself.
- A group chat used for bedtime, school worry, and subjects — without criticism or nitpicking except for safety.
- Cordial, not friends. “You don’t have to like your business partner.”
- Show up together at sports and school; do not share holidays.
- Parallel parenting with a strict plan when conflict is high, because flexibility makes things worse.

No one in this thread asks for an app by name. The repeated unmet need is a way to stay logistical, share safety-critical facts, and not require friendship.

## Priority ranking

Ranked for this thread only, using intensity, specificity, how often it appears, and whether a product could realistically help.

1. **Unwilling coparent withholding information, including medication, inside a high-conflict or abusive dynamic.** Highest intensity and specificity. A shared log or court-friendly record might help the parent who wants transparency; it will not create cooperation, and it is dangerous to design as if both sides will use it in good faith.

2. **Week-one-to-year-one mess: needs-only communication, divorce not even accepted, cannot talk about “how it should be.”** Most repeated pain here. A staged model (parallel now, revisit later) matches what people are already doing. Forcing a “coparenting workspace” in week 3 does not.

3. **Coparenting in name only when one parent has moved far away and shows up 1–2 times a year.** Specific and solvable only at the margins (remote school/medical updates, visit planning). The labor imbalance is the real problem; software will not move someone 8 hours closer.

4. **Idea vs. reality: people think coparenting means matching homes, then fight when it doesn’t.** High leverage for education and onboarding (coparenting vs. parallel parenting; agree on bedtime/screen time or stop pretending). Lower emotional heat than items 1–3.

5. **Rules blurring across houses once parents live apart.** Mentioned as an early fact (“those lines have blurred”) and as “challenging” in parallel-parenting homes. Real, but commenters also treat some inconsistency as the price of less conflict.

6. **Adults who will not put the child above dislike of the ex.** Important diagnosis, too vague in this thread to build on. No incident-level quotes.
