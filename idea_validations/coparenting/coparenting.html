The before

You are not failing at coparenting. You are not in that place yet.

Week three. It is messy. Your ex is on needs-only communication, and so are you. The divorce application might not even be accepted yet. At the start, someone talked about being awesome coparents. You just don’t think you are in that place. You are figuring out your own routines, trying to keep the kids loved and cared for, and hoping for a stretch of time without conflict. Opening a conversation about how it should be feels impossible, because it is.

So you do the thing that actually protects their stability. Parallel parenting, without the fight. You do your thing at your house. They do theirs at theirs. Minimal communication, reserved for the necessities. You might still want to coparent. Both of you might. The split is too raw to force it, so you focus on the kids’ stability and comfort, and on the share of care you actually have — even when that share is nothing like 50/50. “Come back in a year” sounds like a brush-off. It is also the only honest timeline.

Sometimes the word itself is the lie. They moved hours away and show up once or twice a year, while you still send the medical and school updates. Or they have decided that anything on their time is not yours to know, including medication. That is not a scheduling annoyance, and it is not a friendship problem. A product that only works when both adults log in, in good faith, has already failed you.

Why the last solution failed

The idea of coparenting and the reality are massively skewed. People are sold two homes, one team, matching bedtimes, matching screen time, a shared mood. There is a reason you could not stay together. Parenting approaches and styles may have played a part. If you do not agree on the basics, you are not coparenting badly. You are parallel parenting. Different routines are challenging. They can also mean less conflict. The lines have already blurred. Pretending they have not is how the last fix failed — the app, the relative, the group chat that turned into criticism. They all required a partnership you do not have, a second subscription you cannot force, and a friendship nobody is offering. Putting the kids above the dislike is the point. Performing a team is not.

The after

Imagine the logistics moving, and the peace treaty taken off the table.

Relief

The big talk is closed on purpose.

For 90 days, communication stays needs-only. Pickup. School. Medication. Care. Nobody is grading your tone or asking you to articulate the whole future in one message. Space is part of the plan, not a delay you have to apologize for.

Steadiness

The kids are loved in both places, without a performance.

Cordial, not friends. You can show up where it matters and not share the holiday. House rules can differ. Matching homes is not the measure of whether they are cared for. Less conflict can be worth the inconsistency.

Agency

The record holds if you are the only one here.

If you are doing the parenting while they visit once or twice a year, you are not waiting on their mood or their subscription. What you sent is written down. What they never acknowledged is written down. What happened in their house is not invented, and not watched.

A better way

Mode, not morale.

The better way is not a nicer shared calendar and not a lecture about putting the kids first. It is a state you can name. Raw, while it is new. Parallel, as the default — and maybe forever. A few joint decisions, only where you both actually agree. Cooperative, only if both of you opt in later. Parallel is not the downgrade you get when coparenting fails. It is the honest operating system for a split that is still a split.

The system

Introducing Parallel

The parallel-first system for separated homes. It starts in needs-only mode, keeps each house private, and moves the facts that can hurt a child — medication, school, pickup — without requiring friendship, a matching bedtime, or a second willing adult.

Built to be handed across a desk at the first meeting, not discovered in an app store at midnight. Our price does not depend on you becoming cooperative. You are allowed to stay in parallel.

1

Name the mode.

Parallel for now. A 90-day freeze on “how it should be.” Cooperative stays locked until both of you opt in. Skipping the revisit is a valid result, not a missed assignment.

2

Move necessities only.

Pickup, school, a medication change. Short templates. No criticism thread hiding in the same chat as a dose. House rules stay in your house. They are not synced, and they are not a scoreboard.

3

Keep the record if you are alone in it.

You log what you sent, and whether it was acknowledged. Silence is a timestamp, not a story about their house. They get a free door. They cannot cancel yours. You can hand the chronology to a family lawyer. That is a record of attempts, not a promise a court will treat it a certain way.

A note from us

We did not build another app that opens on a shared vision board. The parents who shaped Parallel were clear. Time without conflict is the point. Keep the kids loved and cared for. Figure out your own routines. Come back in a year if you want to. Some of them were three weeks in, transactional and raw, with paperwork not even accepted. Some were already doing all the parenting while the other name on the order lived a day’s drive away.

Parallel is that interval, made into a system. It will not grade you for refusing to match screen time. It will not die because the other parent will not pay. And it will not offer you a window into their house, their phone, or your child as a messenger.

If a child is unsafe, this is the wrong tool. Contact a family lawyer, a mediator, or child-protection services. A message log does not make dosing safe, does not lessen abuse, and will not create a careful or willing partner. Parallel records what you sent. It does not watch the other home, and it should not be used to ask a child what the other parent did.

Before the next handoff

Set the mode while the only honest topic is still what the kids need tonight.

The big conversation can wait. The pickup time cannot. Put “how it should be” on a 90-day freeze, keep the channel needs-only, and revisit later — or don’t. No scarcity trick. Just the next exchange.
