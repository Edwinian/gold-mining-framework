The before

The script arrives unreadable. The booth does not care.

01

Messy source, live clock

The sides land as a deck export, a forwarded email, or a PDF built for a client review—not a booth. Tiny type, two columns, a logo lockup, and a legal line buried under the color block. You zoom until the words are readable and the line breaks are gone. Unreadable in the booth is not a script. It is a missed take.

02

Words, with the performance stripped out

You run the file through whatever transcription tool you already pay for. You get words. You do not get the pause, the alt, or the disclaimer you are not allowed to paraphrase. Emphasis is gone. The brand is spelled three ways. Nothing tells you how they want it said, and the expensive failure—a mispronounced name on a paid spot—is still sitting in the copy.

03

The session is faster than your formatting

Auditions and live-directed sessions move faster than manual formatting. You highlight, you paste, you ask which version is current. Revisions multiply across actor, director, and client. By take four nobody is sure which file is canonical, and you already lost a take to overtime or to legal copy you didn’t see coming.

Why the usual fixes still cost you a take

You already tried the obvious things. They fail for a boring reason: they were never built to hand you a cold read.

  • Pasting into a doc makes you the formatter, on the audition clock.
  • Highlighting a PDF does not separate alt takes from the hero read.
  • Generic transcripts drop emphasis, pauses, alt lines, and legal reads. They produce words.
  • Email threads multiply versions. None of them is a revision-safe booth card.

Horizontal tools already won generic transcription. They were not asked to think in clocks, disclaimers, or a one-page cold read. That fight is not this one.
